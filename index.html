CMU · ROBOT AUTONOMY · MUJOCO SIMULATION

Franka Robot Autonomy — Collision-Aware Motion Planning

Built joint-space planning pipelines for a 7-DOF Franka Panda manipulator in MuJoCo. The project brings together geometric kinematics, collision checking, sampling-based planning, and simulated trajectory playback.

Robot model & collision geometry
Modeled Franka links and obstacles as oriented bounding boxes. Used the Separating Axis Theorem (SAT) for geometric collision checks in configuration space.
RRT motion planning
Implemented a single-tree RRT extension strategy with 10% goal bias, followed by randomized waypoint shortcuts validated against collisions.
PRM roadmap planning
Constructed a roadmap from 1,000 sampled configurations and collision-validated its joint-space edges for subsequent path queries.
Simulated playback
Integrated joint-space trajectory interpolation with gravity-compensated PD torque control for playback in MuJoCo.
Supplementary kinematics
Explored weighted damped-least-squares inverse kinematics using the manipulator Jacobian.

CARNEGIE MELLON UNIVERSITY · 16-665 ROBOT MOBILITY

F1TENTH Autonomous Racing

Implemented and evaluated ROS 2/C++ autonomy modules for the F1TENTH platform, from reactive LiDAR behaviors to waypoint tracking and sampling-based motion planning. The speed-planning extension compares curvature-aware Pure Pursuit against a constant-speed baseline on the same Levine simulation setup.

Perception & control
LiDAR/odometry time-to-collision emergency braking; PID wall following from two-ray wall estimates; and reactive Follow-the-Gap navigation with scan smoothing and obstacle safety bubbles.
Planning & tracking
Waypoint-based Pure Pursuit plus local LiDAR occupancy-grid RRT and RRT* planning, with path and target visualization in RViz.
Recorded lap-time result
Curvature-aware speed planning: 12.00 s baseline → 9.59 s, a 20.1% reduction. The comparison uses supplied logs from the same Levine setup; the measurements were not regenerated for this page.
Tracking error
Maximum error: 0.466 m → 0.458 m (both below the 0.5 m threshold). Mean error increased from 0.091 m to 0.126 m, so the result is a lap-time gain without exceeding the maximum-error limit—not an improvement in every accuracy measure.

EMBEDDED SYSTEMS & IOT · A.Y. 2020–2021

IoT Smart Car Parking System

A PICT Semester II project prototype for monitoring parking availability and presenting it through a mobile app and a web interface. I built the complete hardware prototype and wrote its embedded code.

Problem
Drivers spend time and fuel searching for open parking. The project explored showing space availability before arrival and representing a way to reserve or cancel a booking.
Prototype architecture
An Arduino Uno R3 reads six IR sensors for the four parking bays and entry/exit gates, drives two servo motors, and updates a 16×2 LCD. A NodeMCU ESP8266 receives the Arduino data over serial and sends status over Wi-Fi.
Mobile status
The NodeMCU connects the parking-slot display to a Blynk mobile app, where I configured the parking-slot status view.
Website workflow
The team’s web concept used HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Bootstrap, and Firebase Realtime Database to show parking data and describe booking and cancellation flows. The report lists credential validation and date/time synchronization as future work, so this is presented as a project workflow—not a production booking service.
My contribution
Built and integrated every hardware component in the complete prototype and wrote the embedded code that runs the system.
Tools & hardware
Arduino Uno R3, NodeMCU ESP8266, six IR sensors, two servo motors, 16×2 LCD, Blynk, Firebase Realtime Database, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and Bootstrap.

WIRELESS EMBEDDED SYSTEMS · AUG 2024

LoRa-Based Precision Irrigation System

A distributed agricultural monitoring and irrigation-control prototype connecting field sensing, long-range wireless communication, and a live web dashboard.

Challenge
Monitor agricultural conditions and support irrigation decisions across fields where continuous Wi-Fi access may not be practical.
Distributed architecture
Designed field and receiver nodes around two ESP32-WROOM controllers and SX1278 Ra-02 LoRa transceivers operating at 433 MHz. The field node gathers readings and sends them to a receiver connected to the local monitoring software. The guide describes up to 15 km as a target communication range, not a verified field-test result.
Sensing & actuation
Integrated soil-moisture and DHT11 temperature/humidity sensing with a relay-driven water-pump interface. This connects environmental measurements to an irrigation-control mechanism.
Live monitoring
Built a Python/Flask web dashboard with AJAX updates to display incoming sensor information and support remote monitoring.
Data-driven exploration
Explored machine-learning workflows for pump-state prediction from environmental features, including model training and evaluation. Also investigated OpenCV and NPK-related crop assessment. These are exploratory components; the available project evidence does not establish autonomous ML-driven pump operation or validated crop-health predictions.
Outcome & recognition
Documented the distributed sensing, LoRa communication, monitoring software, and irrigation-control prototype in a 22-step build guide. The project was Runner-up at the PICT TechFiesta Hackathon.
Tools & skills
ESP32-WROOM, SX1278 Ra-02, 433 MHz LoRa, Arduino C/C++, Python, Flask, AJAX, soil-moisture sensing, DHT11, relay and pump control, scikit-learn, ONNX, OpenCV, wireless communication, and embedded systems.

MOBILE ROBOTICS · JUL 2023 — MAR 2024

Autonomous Mobile Manipulator

Originally presented as the Mecanum-Drive Robotic Arm Manipulator, this project is a custom-built omnidirectional robot combining holonomic mobility, perception, and robotic-arm manipulation—from mechanical CAD and simulation through physical prototyping.

Challenge
Explore how a mobile robot can extend a fixed manipulator's workspace by combining indoor navigation, visual object identification, and arm motion on one compact platform.
Mechanical design
Designed a four-wheel mecanum chassis with a custom 3 mm aluminum frame and 2 mm arm linkages. The arm included a machined, bearing-supported rotating base and a servo-actuated gripper.
Navigation & perception
Integrated an RPLIDAR A1, camera, IMU, and TF-Luna distance sensor. Used SLAM Toolbox and Nav2 for mapping and navigation workflows, with OpenCV ArUco detection for visual marker identification.
Odometry exploration
Investigated floor-facing optical-mouse motion sensing for planar displacement, paired with MPU6050 yaw measurements. This explored an alternative to relying only on mecanum-wheel motion estimates, which can be affected by wheel slip.
Simulation & hardware
Created the robot model in Fusion 360 and converted its geometry into URDF. Developed and evaluated mapping, navigation, and MoveIt 2 arm-planning workflows in Gazebo and RViz; separately integrated the physical prototype with Raspberry Pi–ESP32 communication, motor drivers, servo electronics, and power distribution.
Outcome
Demonstrated the physical robot with joystick-based mecanum control, sensor integration, and visual marker identification. The project received First Prize in Instructables' All Things Pi Contest. Simulation planning workflows and physical demonstrations are described separately; an end-to-end autonomous pick-and-place mission on hardware is not claimed.
Tools & skills
ROS 2 Humble, Python, C++, Fusion 360, URDF, Gazebo, RViz, SLAM Toolbox, Nav2, MoveIt 2, OpenCV, ArUco, Raspberry Pi, ESP32, embedded motor control, and mechanical design.

EMBEDDED ROBOTICS · JAN — JUN 2022

IoT-Enabled Surveillance Robot

Originally presented as the IoT-Based Smart Security Surveillance Robot, this Raspberry Pi-powered mobile platform brought robot teleoperation, live video, camera positioning, and sensor feedback into a browser-based interface.

Challenge
Fixed surveillance cameras have limited viewpoints. The project explored a mobile alternative that operators could move remotely while monitoring live video and nearby obstacles.
System design
Built around a Raspberry Pi 3B and a custom 3D-printed chassis, with two DC geared drive motors controlled through an L293D motor driver.
Browser-based teleoperation
Developed a Python and Flask interface for Wi-Fi robot movement control, live camera viewing, camera positioning, and sensor-status feedback, with a login mechanism supporting multiple users.
Vision & sensing
Integrated a Raspberry Pi Camera Module v1.3 on an SG90 servo mount for remotely adjustable camera orientation across an approximately 180° pan range, plus three IR proximity sensors for obstacle detection and operator feedback.
Mechanical & electronics integration
Designed the chassis in Fusion 360, prepared components for 3D printing, and integrated the Raspberry Pi, motors, driver, camera servo, IR sensors, and power hardware. The electronics design also used Autodesk EAGLE.
Outcome & recognition
Delivered a functional web-controlled prototype with live video and sensor feedback. The project received the Judges' Prize in the Autodesk Instructables Electronics Contest and was documented in a 42-step build guide.
Tools & skills
Raspberry Pi 3B, Python, Flask, Wi-Fi, Raspberry Pi Camera v1.3, SG90 servo, IR sensors, L293D, DC motors, Fusion 360, Autodesk EAGLE, Ultimaker Cura, 3D printing, web teleoperation, and embedded-system integration.
